Walk downhill.
But wisely.

Gradient descent is the rule that lets a model improve: take a small step toward lower error, measure again, and repeat.

Intuition

A blindfolded hiker

Imagine standing on a foggy hillside. You cannot see the valley, but you can feel which direction slopes downward. Gradient descent repeatedly follows that local downhill direction.

new position = old position − learning rate × slope

“Learning rate” is the size of each step. Too timid and you crawl; too bold and you leap past the valley.

Common misconception

Lowest nearby ≠ lowest everywhere

The gradient tells you the best direction right now. On complicated landscapes, it can settle in a local valley. That is why initialization and optimizer design matter.

Worked example

Minimize x²

At x = 4, the slope is 8. With rate 0.1, the next point is 4 − 0.1 × 8 = 3.2. Repeating this makes x approach zero—the minimum.
